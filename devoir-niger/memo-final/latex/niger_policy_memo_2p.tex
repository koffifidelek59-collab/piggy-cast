\begin{graybox}{Key message}
\textbf{Under current global policies, days of dangerous heat in Niger would triple by 2100, from about 31 a
year to 91; holding warming below 2\degC{} would cap them at about 42.} In a country where farming and herding
employ over 80\,\% of workers, mostly outdoors, this is the largest avoidable loss. Severe drought, however,
spreads to almost the whole territory by 2030 in every scenario: \textbf{Niger must press for a rapid global
phase-out of emissions and secure finance to adapt now.}
\end{graybox}

\section{1. Why the stakes are high}
Agriculture and livestock generate almost 40\,\% of GDP and depend overwhelmingly on rainfall
(\WBtwentyfour). Shocks are already deadly: the 2024 floods killed 339 people and affected more than
1.1 million (\AFP). Yet Niger's share of global emissions is negligible, so its losses can only be limited by
others' cuts.

\section{3. What limiting warming would change for Niger}
A below-2\degC{} world would spare Niger about 49 days of dangerous heat a year by 2100 and cut the
intensification of extreme rainfall from +43\,\% to +17\,\%; a 1.5\degC{} pathway would keep heat near today's
level (32 days). Climate change could reduce annual GDP by 2.2\,\% to 11.9\,\% by 2050 (\WBtwentytwo). Drought,
by contrast, becomes the norm in every scenario, which makes adaptation urgent: drought-tolerant millet and
sorghum, water harvesting, early warning and protected herder mobility. The real danger is a cascade: a hot,
dry year → failed harvests and pastures → higher grain prices and distress sales of livestock → malnutrition,
debt and farmer--herder tensions → emergency spending that crowds out investment.

\begin{graybox}{Recommended negotiating position}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Defend the 1.5\degC{} goal and NDCs consistent with a rapid fossil-fuel phase-out.
  \item Tie Niger's support to scaled-up adaptation finance with simplified access for LDCs.
  \item Use the Loss and Damage Fund, with the African Group and the LDC Group.
\end{enumerate}
\end{graybox}

\section{4. Confidence and limits}
Heat projections are robust in direction but wide in size (59 to 134 days in 2100). The drought indicator
saturates, and the tool does not model millet or sorghum. These are hazard projections, not forecasts; given
Niger's vulnerability, real impacts are likely to be larger.
